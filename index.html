THE DATA CHECK: 171,000 Net Migration Baseline

The Office for National Statistics (ONS) provisionally estimated UK long-term international net migration at 171,000 in the year ending December 2025.

Net Migration = Inflow minus Outflow. ONS estimated 813,000 long-term immigrants entered, while approximately 642,000 long-term emigrants left the country.

JOHN SMYTHE CHECK:

171,000 is a net statistical balance. It does not reflect total gross arrivals, it is distinct from raw asylum claim volumes, and it does not serve as an active count of the unauthorised population.
Original source: ONS Dataset →

ASYLUM SYSTEM METRICS

Home Office statistics record 85,891 people claiming asylum in the UK in the year ending June 2026. Data records 40% (33,949) as small-boat arrivals, 12% (10,348) in other identified illegal-entry categories, and 41% (34,999) as having previously held a valid visa before claiming asylum.

DATA RULE: NET MIGRATION ≠ ASYLUM CLAIMS • ASYLUM CLAIMS ≠ ILLEGAL ENTRY

An asylum claim marks entrance into a legal determination tracking system, not a total count of people living unlawfully in the UK.
Original source: Home Office Statistics →

THE UNAUTHORISED POPULATION SCOPE

There is no verified live count giving the exact current number of people living in the UK without lawful immigration status. Estimates vary substantially depending on definitions.

JOHN SMYTHE FINDING:

The exact number is NOT DEFINITIVELY ESTABLISHED by verified live records. Historical baseline reference figures include a Greater London Authority study (674,000 to 809,000) and Pew Research Center (700,000 to 900,000) based on 2017 profiles. They are not current counts.

Political Estimates: Reform UK states it exceeds 1 million. Restore Britain estimates up to 2 million. These are political calculations, not verified counts.

Independent source: Oxford Migration Observatory →

Questions From Britain

John Smythe is putting the same 10 baseline questions to all six political parties. Each party will be assessed against its own published immigration policies, with the same standards of evidence, transparency and accountability. Questions that do not apply to a party's proposals will be recorded as not applicable.

  1. How many people would your immigration policy remove during one full parliamentary term?
  2. Of those people, how many are currently legally resident?
  3. What is the estimated total implementation cost?
  4. Which countries have agreed, or indicated willingness, to accept the people you propose returning?
  5. How much additional detention capacity would be required?
  6. Which UK laws would need to be repealed or amended?
  7. Which international obligations would need to be left, renegotiated or interpreted differently?
  8. What appeal and judicial-review rights would remain?
  9. How would the policy deal with British spouses, British children and mixed-nationality families?
  10. Will you publish the datasets, assumptions and calculations behind your estimates and costings?
